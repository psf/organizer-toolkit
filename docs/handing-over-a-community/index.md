# Handing Over a Community

The ultimate measure of your leadership is not how well the community runs while you hold it, but how well it runs after you let go. A community that collapses when you leave was never fully built. One that thrives without you is your real legacy.

Plan for this from the beginning, not the end. Handover is not an event you scramble through in your final weeks; it is a discipline you practice from day one by sharing accounts, documenting decisions, and growing other leaders while you are still fully present.


## Why Succession Matters

- **It protects the community.** Life is unpredictable. Illness, a new job, a move, or simple exhaustion can pull you away with little warning. A community ready to be handed over survives these shocks. One built entirely around you does not.

- **It grows other leaders.** Preparing to hand over forces you to develop people, delegate real authority, and step back. That is not a loss; that is how leaders are made.

- **It frees you.** Knowing the community can run without you is what lets you rest and take breaks, and it is one of your best defenses against [burnout](../burnout/index.md). Being irreplaceable is not a compliment, it is a trap.


## Preparing Long Before You Leave

- **Never be the only one with the keys.** From early on, make sure at least one other trusted person can access every critical account. This is why the [Current Community Organizers](../current-organizers/index.md) guidance recommends a shared community email address and a password manager. It is at handover that all of that pays off.

- **Document as you go, not at the end.** Keep a living handover document recording every account and who owns it, your regular sponsors and contacts, your venue relationships, your event checklists, and the reasoning behind key decisions. The context in your head is the most valuable and most easily lost asset the community has.

- **Grow your successor in plain sight.** Give rising leaders visible, meaningful roles well before you plan to leave, so that when the moment comes the community already trusts them and they already know how things work.


## The Handover Checklist

When it is time to hand over, work through this deliberately with the incoming person.

- **Transfer every account.** Website, social media, email, registration platforms, analytics, and finances. Confirm each one actually works in their hands before you step back, then remove your personal access where appropriate.

- **Hand over the money cleanly.** Bank access, sponsor agreements, outstanding invoices, and financial records. Leave the finances transparent and fully documented. Nothing sours a handover faster than tangled money.

- **Introduce the relationships personally.** Warmly connect your successor to sponsors, venues, partner groups, and any PSF contacts who matter. A personal introduction transfers trust in a way no document can.

- **Walk through the living document together.** Do not just send it. Sit with them, explain the parts that need context, and answer their questions while you still can.

- **Announce the transition publicly and generously.** Tell the community clearly who is now leading, express real confidence in them, and thank the people who built things alongside you. A clear, warm public handover gives your successor the community's blessing to lead.


## After You Hand Over

- **Stay available, but do not hover.** Offer to answer questions and be a sounding board, then genuinely let the new leaders lead, including when they do things differently than you would have. A predecessor who cannot let go is a burden, not a resource.

- **Champion them from the sidelines.** Speak well of them publicly, celebrate their wins, and resist the urge to relitigate their choices. Your visible confidence steadies their leadership.

- **Let yourself feel it, and let yourself rest.** Handing over something you built with love is genuinely hard. Give yourself grace. The truest sign of a community well built is that it outgrows the person who started it.
