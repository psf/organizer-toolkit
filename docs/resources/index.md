# Resources

A collection of links, tools, and templates that organizers commonly need. If you find something missing that helped you, contributions to this toolkit are welcome.

## Get Your Community and Events Listed

Being listed is how newcomers, travelling Pythonistas, and potential speakers find you.

- [Local User Groups Worldwide](https://wiki.python.org/moin/LocalUserGroups): add your community to the global list.
- [Submitting an event to the Python events calendars](https://wiki.python.org/moin/PythonEventsCalendar#Submitting_an_Event): gets your event onto the calendars shown on python.org.
- [Python Conferences List](https://wiki.python.org/moin/PythonConferences): the most complete list of conferences worldwide.
- [pycon.org](https://www.pycon.org/): national and regional PyCon conferences.
- [PyData events](https://pydata.org/): data science and scientific computing focused events.

## Places to Ask for Help

Almost every problem you will hit has already been solved by another organizer.

- [Python conferences mailing list](https://mail.python.org/mailman/listinfo/conferences): where conference and event organizers help each other. The single most useful list to join.
- [Python Discord](https://discord.gg/4Hm36PPgpG)
- [Python Community Discourse](https://discuss.python.org/)
- [Help and General Contact](https://www.python.org/about/help/)
- grants@pyfound.org for questions about funding and grant applications.
- psf-trademarks@python.org for permission to use PSF trademarks such as "PyCon".
- conduct-wg@python.org to reach the PSF Code of Conduct Working Group.

## Funding and Financial Support

### Grants

The PSF supports Python related programs through its [Grants Program](https://www.python.org/psf/grants/). The programme is genuinely global: in 2023 the PSF distributed grants to 174 groups across 52 countries.

- [Submit a grant proposal](https://psfmember.org/grant-proposals/). You may need to create a free account on [psfmember.org](https://psfmember.org/) first.
- Apply early. Review takes time, and applications are stronger when they are specific about what the money achieves and who it reaches.
- Questions go to grants@pyfound.org.

Grants can also cover your [Meetup Pro fees](../current-organizers/index.md#how-do-i-ask-the-psf-to-cover-meetup-fees).

### Local and in-kind support

You can consider reaching out to local organizations for funding, in terms of space, food, internet etc. In-kind support is often easier to secure than cash, and it avoids the administrative burden of handling money:

- Venue space from universities, coworking spaces, hackerspaces, and local companies.
- Food and refreshments, sometimes sponsored per-event by a local business.
- Printing, banners, and swag.
- Cloud credits and software licenses, which many vendors offer to user groups.
- Asking a sponsor to pay a supplier directly rather than transferring money to you.

See [Legal Status, Registration, and Fiscal Sponsorship](../current-organizers/index.md#legal-status-registration-and-fiscal-sponsorship) for how to handle money before your group is a registered entity.

## PSF Fiscal Sponsorship

In addition to grants, meetups and conferences may receive ongoing logistical support from the PSF through the Fiscal Sponsoree program. This includes back-office support such as accounting, event insurance, and non-profit status. Find out more at <https://www.python.org/psf/fiscal-sponsorees/>

Two things to check before you plan around it: the PSF is **not currently accepting new fiscal sponsoree requests**, and in practice the programme has supported groups and projects based in the United States. If you are outside the US, plan for a local solution instead.

## Tools Organizers Commonly Use

None of these are required, and none are endorsements. Use whatever your community already uses.

**Event pages and registration**

- [Meetup](https://meetup.com), Eventbrite, Luma for event listings and RSVPs.
- [Pretix](https://pretix.eu/) and [Tito](https://ti.to/) for ticketed events.
- [Pretalx](https://pretalx.com/) for calls for proposals and scheduling, widely used by Python conferences.

**Design and communication**

- [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) for flyers and social graphics.
- A shared password manager so accounts survive organizer turnover. See [Account & Password Management](../current-organizers/index.md#account-password-management).
- Whatever chat platform your community actually uses locally, whether that is WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Slack, or Matrix.

**Websites**

- GitHub Pages or GitLab Pages for free static hosting.
- Remember that you can request a free `pycon.org` subdomain for an authorized PyCon. See [Conference Organizing](../conference-organizing/index.md).

## Templates Worth Building Once

These are not provided here, but writing them once will save you enormous time. Keep them in a shared folder your co-organizers can access.

- An event checklist, reviewed and updated after every event.
- A call for proposals or call for speakers announcement.
- A speaker confirmation email, including timings and what to expect on the day.
- A sponsorship prospectus and tiered package list.
- A sponsor thank-you and post-event report.
- A post-event feedback form.
- A budget spreadsheet, including a contingency line.
- A volunteer role description sheet.
- A handover document listing every account and who owns it.

## PSF Membership

By [becoming a member](https://www.python.org/psf/membership/), you tell us and the world that you are a part of the PSF and support our work, and as one of the classes of voting members, you add your voice to the future direction of the Foundation.

As a community organizer, you can nominate yourself or community members to become Fellows at the PSF. Fellows are members who have been nominated for their extraordinary efforts and impact upon Python, the community, and the broader Python ecosystem. Visit the [PSF's Fellow Membership page](https://www.python.org/psf/fellows/) to nominate a Fellow or learn more about the process.
